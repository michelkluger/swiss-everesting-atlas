How this was measured, the time model, and ways to stretch the day

How it was measured

  • Lifts: OpenStreetMap cable cars, gondolas and funiculars, plus the Uetliberg, Rigi and Pilatus railways. Chairlifts are left out because almost all are ski-only.
  • Rise: swisstopo heights at both stations.
  • Trail: BRouter mountain-hiking route from the valley station to the top station.
  • Travel: transport.opendata.ch, weekday morning.

The time model

Climb time per lap uses DIN 33466, the standard the alpine clubs use for hiking times: the larger of distance time and ascent time, plus half the smaller. The rates come from your sliders.

Laps = ⌈8 848 ÷ rise⌉. Each descent adds the ride, a wait (half the departure interval, about 1 min for gondolas) and 2 min to change over.

Lab work on vertical-km racing (Giovanelli et al. 2016) puts the lowest energy cost per metre climbed at about 20–30° (36–58 %). No trail here averages that steep, so steeper is always better.

Ways to stretch the day

  • Bookend laps: walk down on foot before the lift opens and after it closes.
  • Go on a weekday in June or July for the most daylight and no queues.
  • With a Halbtax, a day GA (Spartageskarte) makes every descent free on GA-valid lifts and covers the train there.
  • Official Everesting run/hike rules allow lift descents.
